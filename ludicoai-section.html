Learn English from the films themselves.

LudicoAI teaches English through real films. Learners watch any of 1,255 public-domain films with a line-by-line transcript, or a video of their own, and tap any word to see what it means in that scene, hear how it is said, and hear the same word in other accents. 01What a learner does

The same help in two places: inside the films, and on top of every other app.

In the films

Watch, tap, understand

  • A library of 1,255 films, filtered by main accent and by length
  • Tap a word in the line: its meaning in that scene, its pronunciation, clips of the same word in other accents
  • Missed a line? Hear it again and see it written out word by word
  • Import your own videos: who speaks and with which accent is worked out on the phone
Everywhere else

The floating bubble

  • A bubble over other apps explains selected text, in the learner's own language if they wish
  • Short sessions around one film or one scene, mostly on phones: what the closed test showed
  • Core features free; Premium through Google Play raises the usage limits
02The AI pipeline
Who said what
Films are transcribed with speakers separated (whisper-1, gpt-4o-transcribe-diarize), so every line knows its speaker.
Which accent
Accents are identified from ECAPA speaker embeddings, which is what lets a word be heard again in other accents.
On the device
For a learner's own videos, speaker and accent detection run on the phone, with two voice models downloaded once after the learner agrees. A transcription finishes in the background; the next one waits its turn.
Explain and say it
Meanings in context and pronunciation come from the OpenAI API (chat and text-to-speech), in the learner's language.
03A data lake that is never half-published

The library lives in a versioned Parquet lake read with DuckDB. A release is a complete dataset that never changes once written, and learners only ever see one that has been checked.

Immutable releases
Each release lists every file with its rows, bytes and SHA-256 in a manifest.
Atomic channels
CURRENT.public and CURRENT.dev switch only to a release that verifies against its manifest; servers keep serving the last good one until the new one checks out.
Bucketed for DuckDB
Words and transcripts are split into 256 buckets by a stable hash, sorted inside each bucket, so a lookup reads one small file and DuckDB skips row groups.
Works offline
The desktop app falls back from the main tier to a low-cost tier, then to a local index.
A lesson from production

With every transcript in one Parquet file, DuckDB decompressed all 210 MB to open a single film and ran out of memory under the server's 256 MB limit: no film opened. Transcripts are now bucketed by film (schema 2), so opening a film reads a file of a few films; the API still reads schema 1, so a rollback stays possible.
